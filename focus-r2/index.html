      About AGRIOS Focus

      AGRIOS is not a smarter view of the farm; it is an honest one — an instrument that always says where it stands, refuses to speak past its reach, and asks for the farmer's eyes at the edge of what it can see.

      Enter coordinates and Focus reads that ground live from public sources — USGS elevation, USDA soils, NWS weather, Census roads, USGS hydrography — then runs the boundary loop: find the edges, see where they meet, rank what matters now by rules it prints for you. Where the sources genuinely disagree, it draws a ⟨?⟩ and holds the question open. That refusal is the product working, not failing.

      Every claim carries its instrument, its resolution limits, and its date. Name a field, state its bounds (your claim of record, drawn solid), and it becomes a place you can return to. The muted "not connected" tiles are honest empty sockets — soil probes, yield monitors, array telemetry light them up.

      Two kinds of reading, kept visibly distinct: the computed reading (geometry and printed rules, data-support counted, never invented confidence) and the analyst layer — a worked interpretation like the Allerton, Iowa example in the field switcher.
